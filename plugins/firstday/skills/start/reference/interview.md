# The interview

Six questions. One per turn. Up to three follow-ups across the whole interview — and if the budget is tight, the people question gets one first. It is the question people answer thinnest and it has the most valuable file behind it.

The tone is a good first-day conversation with a new chief of staff, not an onboarding form. Ask, listen, acknowledge in a few words, move on.

---

## Q1 — What are you actually responsible for?

> Let's start with the job. Not the title on the org chart — what are you actually responsible for?

**What you're after:** the real remit, in their words. Scope, ownership, what lands on their desk because of who they are.

**Follow up if:** they give you a job title and nothing else. Try *"and what does that mean you're on the hook for?"*

---

## Q2 — What fills a normal week?

> What does a normal week actually look like? Where does the time go?

**What you're after:** the shape of the week. Meetings versus deep work, recurring commitments, the rhythm.

**Follow up if:** the answer is under about ten words. Almost everyone under-answers this one. Try *"walk me through a normal Tuesday"* — the specificity unlocks it.

---

## Q3 — Who do you deal with most?

**Do not give a policy speech before this one.** One short clause inside the question does the job, and the real control is what you do with the answer.

> Who do you deal with most? Six or so names. And for each one, what they need from you — how they like to work, rather than what you make of them, since this ends up written down.

**Protected follow-up.** If the answer is a list of names with no context, spend a follow-up here before anywhere else: *"take the first one — what makes a good interaction with them?"*

**What you're after:** names, relationship, and the working handle — how each one likes to be dealt with, what they care about, what they need.

**Translate, don't transcribe.** When somebody gives you a character judgement, rewrite it as working context and tell them you have. That translation is the whole control — it fires when it is needed and it teaches by doing rather than by warning.

Anything touching performance, pay or health does not go in the file at all. Say so once, lightly, only if it comes up.

---

## Q4 — What are you driving this quarter?

Goes into `About me.md` as a dated section, not its own file.

> What are the three or four things you're actually driving right now? The ones you'd be judged on.

**What you're after:** live priorities with enough detail to be recognisable later. Where each one is up to, if they offer it.

**Follow up if:** they list more than six. Ask which two matter most. Fourteen entries is a to-do list and it goes stale in a fortnight.

---

## Q5 — What would you hand over tomorrow?

> If you could hand one recurring thing off your desk tomorrow, what would it be? The thing where you think, I've done this before.

**What you're after:** the automation candidate, and a strong signal about what they'll actually value.

**Note this one carefully.** It is the task you will offer to do together in Stage 3, and it is the most likely thing to make them a repeat user.

---

## Q6 — How do you want to be talked to?

> Last one. When I come back to you with something — do you want the answer first or the thinking first? Short or thorough? And do you want me to push back when I disagree with you, or just get on with it?

**What you're after:** register, length, directness, appetite for challenge.

**Follow up if:** they say "whatever's best". Try *"think about the last time someone sent you something and you were pleased — what did it look like?"*

---

## Reading the room

**If someone is enjoying it,** let them talk. The interview is often the first time anyone has asked them to describe their own job, and the files are better for it.

**If someone is impatient,** compress. Q1, Q3, Q4 and Q6 are the load-bearing four. Two can be left thin. Five is worth keeping even when rushing, because it decides what you do together in Stage 3.

**If someone gives you something confidential** — a live deal, a person's circumstances, anything they'd not put in an email — do not write it into the files. Say so lightly, once: *"I'll leave that out of the file, but it's useful for now."*
